What the model solves

The solver is a D2Q9 lattice Boltzmann scheme with a Smagorinsky turbulence model, on a grid of 352 × 162 cells covering 26.7 m by 12 m of air (7.6 cm per cell). The ground is a wall moving at flight speed, so ground effect shows up when you lower the height.

  • It is a 2D slice. Wings and canard go in as thin cambered plates. The 10° dihedral, the wing tips, the vertical curtains of the Hargrave cells and the bracing wires are left out.
  • The Reynolds number is thousands of times lower than the real one. The air separates from the surfaces earlier than in real flight, so read the numbers as orders of magnitude.
  • Forces come from momentum exchange at the walls, multiplied by an effective span: 10.4 m for the wing pair (52 m² ÷ two 2.5 m chords) and 3 m for the canard.
  • The propeller is a 2 m strip that accelerates the air rearwards. At 100% it adds a velocity equal to the flight speed to the jet. The model does not close the balance between thrust and drag.
  • Flight speed changes the scale of the forces and of time. The flow pattern depends on the simulated Reynolds number, the angle and the geometry.
